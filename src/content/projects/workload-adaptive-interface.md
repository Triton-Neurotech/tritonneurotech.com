## The idea

EEG estimates a user's mental workload during a demanding control task. When the user becomes overloaded, the system raises its level of automation. The team compares this EEG-triggered adaptation against fixed automation and against behavioral measures such as reaction time.

## Example task

A user drives a rover through an obstacle course while also watching for warning indicators.

- **Low workload:** the user controls steering and speed.
- **High workload:** the rover switches on obstacle avoidance and simplifies the interface.

## How we'll measure it

- Collisions
- Completion time
- Missed warnings
- Unnecessary automation switches
- EEG workload detection compared with behavioral baselines
